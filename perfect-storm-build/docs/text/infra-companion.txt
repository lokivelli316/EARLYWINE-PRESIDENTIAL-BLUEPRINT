1. Concept Summary
Critical Infrastructure Automation Instability is a proposed Perfect Storm ring describing a non-
malicious failure mode in which a physical disturbance degrades the digital, sensing, 
communications, or AI-assisted systems used to understand and manage that disturbance. The 
control layer may then respond to an incomplete, stale, contradictory, or otherwise distorted model 
of reality, causing corrective action to become an additional source of instability.
The concept does not require hostile AI behavior. A model, controller, decision-support 
system, or automation stack can contribute to a dangerous cascade while pursuing its assigned 
objective in good faith.
A sufficiently coupled automated system can become part of the failure simply 
by responding incorrectly, inconsistently, or too aggressively to degraded 
conditions created by the failure itself.

GOVERNING PRINCIPLE
Absence of malicious intent is not evidence of safety. High-consequence systems must remain 
safe when models are wrong, sensors disagree, networks degrade, power quality collapses, 
corrections do not persist, and operators receive incomplete information.
3. The Infrastructure Feedback Architecture
At a high level, many modern infrastructure systems operate as feedback loops:
Physical system state
↓
Sensors / telemetry
↓
Automation or AI-assisted interpretation
↓
Recommendation or control action
↓
Physical system response
↓
New sensor state
↓
Reassessment
↺ loop
The risk emerges when the failure changes the quality of the information feeding the 
controller. The controller is then solving a different problem from the one that physically exists.

4. Brownout / Degraded-State Cascade
A brownout is a useful example because it can create partial failure rather than a clean 
shutdown. Different devices, sensors, controllers, communications links, edge systems, and backup 
systems may fail, reset, or recover at different times.
1. Voltage disturbance / brownout
2. Partial reset, packet loss, timing drift, degraded sensors, or inconsistent failover
3. Incomplete / stale / contradictory operational state
4. AI or automation forms the wrong world-model
5. Corrective recommendation or control action is wrong for the actual state
6. Load, flow, pressure, routing, or other physical conditions shift
7. New alarms and telemetry reflect the downstream effects
8. Automation reassesses the changed system
9. Feedback loop can amplify the original disturbance
5. Cross-Infrastructure Coupling
Critical infrastructure is not a collection of isolated machines. Electricity, 
telecommunications, water, fuel, transportation, healthcare, cloud services, and financial systems 
depend on one another.
 Electrical instability can impair communications, pumping, computing, refrigeration, fuel 
distribution, transportation, and emergency operations.
 Telecommunications loss can impair SCADA visibility, dispatch, remote control, cloud access, 
voice communications, and incident coordination.
 Water-system disruption can affect public health, hospitals, firefighting, cooling, and industrial 
operations.
 Cloud, identity, software-update, vendor, or model dependencies can create common-mode 
failure even where physical facilities are geographically separate.
Different systems can look separate physically while sharing the same control-
plane failure domain.
6. Nuclear and Other High-Consequence Systems
The nuclear case must be framed carefully. This companion does not claim that a generative 
model can take over a nuclear reactor or that a nuclear AI accident has occurred. The U.S. Nuclear 
Regulatory Commission is actively preparing for increased AI use in NRC-regulated activities and has 
studied AI/ML applications in plant operation, maintenance, diagnostics, safety, and risk analysis. 
NRC cybersecurity research also treats autonomous monitoring/control and AI/ML as novel 
technologies that may require new technical and regulatory guidance.
[SUPPORTED] NRC research and strategy establish that AI/ML use in nuclear applications is a real 
regulatory and engineering subject, not a science-fiction premise.
[UNKNOWN] The probability of a compound AI-coupled nuclear accident of the type modeled here 
is not established.
[DESIGN QUESTION] Are the systems used to interpret, explain, prioritize, and respond to an 
emergency independent enough that the emergency cannot corrupt their view of the emergency 
itself?
The final safety principle is stronger than 'the AI should be reliable': the highest-
consequence protective functions should not depend on the same AI, communications, cloud, 
identity, sensor-fusion, or software environment whose degradation may be part of the initiating 
event.

7. Why AI Changes the Risk Model
Observation: AI can aggregate and interpret far more telemetry, logs, alarms, forecasts, 
maintenance records, and external context than one human operator.
Speed: AI-assisted systems can classify and respond on machine timescales, potentially faster than 
humans can detect, understand, escalate, and coordinate.
Correlation: AI can connect variables across systems, which is beneficial when the correlations are 
valid and dangerous when the underlying state is wrong or common-mode corrupted.
Authority: The physical consequence depends less on the model's intelligence than on the 
authority attached to its output: advisory, supervisory, or direct control.
Common-mode dependence: If multiple systems use the same model family, cloud service, 
identity plane, software dependency, or telemetry source, apparent redundancy may be weaker 
than it looks.
8. Gemini Hydra Case 004: Concrete Physical-World Failure Pattern
Case 004 is relevant because it demonstrates the failure class at small scale without 
requiring malicious intent. The preserved Google app AI Mode interaction interpreted ambiguous 
rural-road geometry, promoted an unsupported causal explanation to fact, and then added 
unverified runoff, culvert, washout, creek, and emergency-staging claims. The evidence packet later 
established a real watercourse in the relevant corridor and preserved user-reported washout history. 
The exact generative backend model remained unknown.
[OBSERVED IN CASE ARCHIVE] The route screenshots show multiple turns rather than a single 
continuous road. A watercourse is visible in the relevant corridor. The preserved conversation 
contains confident physical-hazard claims that were not independently verified before being stated.
[NOT ESTABLISHED] The case does not show that Google Maps turn-by-turn routed a vehicle into 
water, that Gemini knew the actual flood state, or that physical harm occurred.
[RELEVANCE] A wrong physical-world model can become a safety problem when a person or 
automated system acts on it. That is the infrastructure-ring mechanism in miniature.

13. Failure Conditions
 The AI or automation layer is trusted beyond the quality of its inputs.
 Partial failure produces a plausible but wrong system picture rather than an obvious total outage.
 Multiple controllers or failover systems act on inconsistent versions of state.
 Corrections, overrides, or updated facts do not persist reliably through the incident.
 Apparent redundancy shares a common cloud, identity, software, model, sensor, or 
communications dependency.

 Machine-speed correction occurs faster than meaningful human comprehension and 
coordination.
 The same degraded infrastructure supports the monitoring, communications, and decision 
systems required to manage the emergency.
 Final protective interlocks are not sufficiently independent of the control-plane failure domain.
14. Defensive Architecture
The defensive objective is not to make AI infallible. It is to make catastrophic consequence 
difficult even when the AI is wrong.
 Deterministic, independently enforced safety interlocks for high-consequence physical actions.
 Explicit authority caps: advisory AI should not silently become control AI.
 Graceful degraded-state modes that treat contradictory or stale telemetry as uncertainty, not 
certainty.
 Independent sensor channels and provenance checks for critical state variables.
 Brownout, failover, partial-network, clock-drift, stale-data, and split-brain testing - not only clean 
outage testing.
 Out-of-band shutdown and recovery paths that do not depend on the same cloud, identity, 
network, or model stack.
 Persistent correction state and explicit operator acknowledgement for safety-critical state 
changes.
 Manual operating procedures that remain executable when cloud, internet, remote SCADA, or AI 
assistance is unavailable.
 Post-containment verification that authority, state, and dependencies have actually returned to 
trusted condition.
15. Limiting Factors and Evidence Boundaries
[SUPPORTED] AI and machine learning are being explored or deployed for real infrastructure 
planning, monitoring, diagnostics, maintenance, optimization, and regulatory use cases.
[SUPPORTED] Utilities and regulators already test degraded communications, cyber resilience, and 
continuity of operations.
[OBSERVED - CASE 004] A conversational AI produced confident unsupported physical-hazard 
reasoning in a real-world environment containing genuine hydrologic hazards.
[NOT ESTABLISHED] No evidence in this companion shows Gemini caused physical harm, caused a 
flood, controlled infrastructure, or acted maliciously.
[NOT ESTABLISHED] No evidence in this companion demonstrates an AI-caused nuclear accident 
or a full cross-sector infrastructure cascade.
[UNKNOWN] The frequency and probability of a full Critical Infrastructure Automation Instability 
cascade are not known.

17. The Ring in One Sentence
Critical Infrastructure Automation Instability is a non-malicious compound-failure 
model in which a physical disturbance degrades the digital state used by AI or 
automation to manage the disturbance, causing incorrect or inconsistent 
corrective actions to feed back into the physical system and potentially amplify 
the original failure across coupled infrastructure.
18. Perfect Storm Principle
AI risk does not require an AI that wants to cause harm. In a sufficiently coupled 
control plane, being confidently wrong at the wrong moment can be enough.
The engineering demand is therefore simple: design the system so that a wrong model cannot 
become the final authority over an irreversible physical consequence.
