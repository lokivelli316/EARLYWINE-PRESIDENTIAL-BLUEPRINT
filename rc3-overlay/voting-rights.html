Voting rights after a felony conviction

The law may have given it back.
Find out before you register.

If you have a felony conviction, do not assume you are permanently barred from voting — and do not assume you are eligible. Rules depend on your state or territory and the details of your sentence. Verify first with an official source. If you are eligible, register or update your registration, confirm it, find your polling place or mail-voting instructions, and vote.

Do not register or cast a ballot until you have confirmed eligibility.Vote.gov warns that registering before you are eligible can result in criminal prosecution. This page does not determine anyone's eligibility and does not ask you to vote for any candidate.
The nationwide path
1

Check the federal voting-after-conviction guide.

Start with Vote.gov's official overview. It explains the broad state-by-state categories and warns that sentence details, probation, parole, fines, restitution, and specific offenses can matter.

Open Vote.gov felony guide ↗
2

Confirm your own case with an election authority.

Use your state or local election office. If completion of your sentence or release conditions is unclear, Vote.gov also recommends contacting your lawyer or court representative. Ask directly whether you are eligible to register now.

Find state/local election office ↗
3

If eligible, register or update your registration.

Use Vote.gov to select your state. It routes you to the official registration methods available there — online, mail, or in person. A prior registration may need to be updated or renewed.

Register through Vote.gov ↗
4

Confirm the registration actually took.

Before Election Day, verify your registration status, name, address, and assigned voting location. Do not rely on an old voter card or memory.

Check voter registration ↗
5

Find how and where you vote.

Check your assigned polling place and hours, or your state's official early/absentee/mail instructions. Polling places can change from one election to the next.

Find polling place ↗
6

Vote only after the eligibility + registration checks are green.

Once your eligibility is confirmed and your registration is active, follow your state's official voting instructions. If anything conflicts, the state/local election office is the authority to resolve it.

Authority & source discipline

What this page will not do

It will not say “felons can vote,” “you got your rights back,” or “register today” without verification. It will not state one national eligibility rule because there isn't one.

What counts

Your state or local election office for current election administration; the relevant court/lawyer when your sentence status or legal conditions are unclear; and official federal portals that route you to those authorities.
